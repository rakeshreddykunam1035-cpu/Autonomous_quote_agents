# Underwriting & Premium Guidelines (Reference Knowledge Base)

## Section 1: Risk Tier Definitions
1.1 Low risk drivers have no prior accidents or citations, 4+ years of driving
experience, and annual mileage under 45K. These drivers qualify for standard
or discounted premiums.

1.2 Medium risk drivers show one moderate risk factor: either a new driver
(under 4 years experience), a young driver (under 25), business use of the
vehicle, or high annual mileage (over 45K). Standard premium applies, no
discount is issued for these accounts.

1.3 High risk drivers have at least one prior accident, one prior citation,
or a combination of two or more moderate risk factors from 1.2. These
accounts should never receive a discount, and should be flagged for
underwriter review if the quoted premium sits more than 15% below the
regional average for that vehicle type.

## Section 2: Premium Adjustment Rules
2.1 Discounts of up to 10% may be offered autonomously to Low risk accounts
with a predicted conversion likelihood below 40%, since the goal is to win
price-sensitive business without eroding margin on customers who were going
to convert anyway.

2.2 No discount should be offered to accounts with a conversion likelihood
already above 60% -- the customer is already inclined to bind, so a discount
here only reduces margin without changing the outcome.

2.3 For Medium risk accounts, a smaller discount band (up to 5%) may be
applied only when Coverage tier is Basic or Balanced. Enhanced coverage
Medium-risk accounts should not be discounted, since the enhanced tier
already reflects a pricing accommodation.

2.4 High risk accounts are never eligible for autonomous discounting.
Any premium adjustment for High risk accounts requires underwriter sign-off
per Section 3.

## Section 3: Escalation Protocol
3.1 A quote should be escalated to a human underwriter when any of the
following hold: (a) risk tier is High and quoted premium is unusually low
for the vehicle/coverage combination, (b) the model's confidence in the
risk tier prediction is below 70%, or (c) household drivers exceed
household vehicles by 2 or more (potential undisclosed household risk).

3.2 Quotes that do not meet escalation criteria but also do not clearly
qualify for auto-approval should be routed to the originating agent for
manual follow-up rather than auto-approved, particularly for re-quoted
policies where the customer has shopped the price multiple times.

3.3 Auto-approval is reserved for Low risk, high-confidence quotes with
no re-quote history and a conversion likelihood that does not require any
premium intervention.

## Section 4: Regional Notes
4.1 Region H has historically shown a slightly higher average premium
across all risk tiers; underwriters should not treat this as anomalous
on its own.

4.2 Business-use vehicles (Veh_Usage = Business) carry inherently higher
claim severity potential and should be weighted toward Medium risk at
minimum, regardless of driver history, when household vehicle count
exceeds 3.
